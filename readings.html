Reading

Everything on this list is free — no paywall, no registration, no email capture

Every link here was opened and checked rather than remembered. Where a canonical text sits behind a paywall, an open substitute was found instead, and where no good free source exists the gap is stated rather than filled with something weak.

Nothing on this list is compulsory. The Start here entry for each session is the one to read if you read only one, and most run to twenty minutes or less. The Go deeper entries are for the practitioners in the room and for afterwards.

If a link has died

These were verified on 30 September 2026. The web being what it is, one will eventually rot. Tell us and we will replace it — and in the meantime the title and author are given in full precisely so the piece can be found again.

Day 1 — Strategy, value, and what AI actually changed

1.1 · Competing on analytics

Start here · The Data Asset: Databases, Business Intelligence, and Competitive Advantage
Gallaugher — Information Systems: A Manager’s Guide, ch. 11 (open textbook, CC BY-NC-SA) · ~25 min
The open equivalent of Davenport, built on the same Wal-Mart and Harrah’s cases. Read 11.1 to 11.4, then 11.7 and 11.8.

Go deeper · Data in Action: Data-Driven Decision Making in U.S. Manufacturing
Brynjolfsson & McElheran — US Census Bureau working paper CES 16-06 (2016) · ~45 min
The evidence behind the slogan: about 3% higher value-added, but only alongside skilled staff and delegated decisions — and the advantage erodes as rivals learn.

1.2 · The KPI tree

Start here · Profitability Ratios and the DuPont Method
OpenStax — Principles of Finance, 6.6 · ~15 min
DuPont is the original driver tree: one P&L outcome split into three factors that different functions actually own.

Go deeper · Building Less Flawed Metrics: Dodging Goodhart and Campbell’s Laws
Manheim — MPRA Paper 98288 (2020) · ~45 min
Turns Goodhart’s law from a warning into design guidance — which is what you need before a KPI tree meets a bonus scheme.

Also · Developing Process Safety Indicators
Health and Safety Executive — HSG254 (2006) · ~20 min
The best free treatment of leading versus lagging indicators, written for senior managers. Part 1 is enough.

1.3 · Sizing the prize

Start here · From Nobel Prize to Project Management: Getting Risks Right
Flyvbjerg — Project Management Journal 37(3) (2006) · ~25 min
Why business cases are overstated — optimism bias and strategic misrepresentation — and reference class forecasting as the defensible answer.

Go deeper · The Green Book: Appraisal and Evaluation in Central Government
HM Treasury — 2026 edition, Open Government Licence · ~35 min
Chapters 4 to 6 and 9. Switching values are the sharpest tool here: state how wrong your assumption would have to be before the decision flips.

Also · Divide and Conquer
Mahajan — The Art of Insight in Science and Engineering, ch. 1 (MIT OpenCourseWare) · ~40 min
The canonical free treatment of Fermi estimation: breaking an unknowable quantity into knowable factors.

1.4 · What AI actually changed

Start here · The productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence
Noy & Zhang — CEPR VoxEU (2023) · ~12 min
A randomised experiment on exactly this audience’s work. The floor rises further than the ceiling, which matters most if you manage analysts.

Go deeper · Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier
Dell’Acqua et al. — Organization Science (2026), CC BY 4.0 · ~45 min
758 consultants. Inside the frontier, faster and better; beyond it, 19% less likely to be right. The boundary has to be found, not assumed.

Also · Generative AI at Work
Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond — arXiv 2304.11771 · ~60 min
5,172 support agents. Gains concentrate among the less experienced — an organisational-design finding, not a tooling one.

1.5 · Where the data lives

Start here · Understand star schema and the importance for Power BI
Microsoft — Microsoft Learn · ~20 min
Dimensional modelling in plain prose. Explains why ‘can you just add a column’ is sometimes trivial and sometimes a quarter’s work.

Go deeper · Lakehouse: A New Generation of Open Platforms
Armbrust, Ghodsi, Xin & Zaharia — CIDR 2021 · ~30 min
The clearest free account of why the data estate looks the way it does. The first two pages alone are worth it.

Also · Understanding ELT: Extract, Load, Transform
dbt Labs — dbt blog · ~15 min
Why analytics work moved from IT projects to analyst-owned work. Ignore the closing sales pitch.

1.6 · Lab 1 · The use-case charter

Start here · Designing great data products: The Drivetrain Approach
Howard, Zwemer & Loukides — O’Reilly Radar (2012) · ~25 min
Forces the two questions people skip: what is the objective, and what lever can we actually pull? The difference between a dashboard and a decision.

Go deeper · The Aqua Book: Guidance on Producing Quality Analysis for Government
HM Treasury — gov.uk (2025) · ~30 min
Chapters 5 and 6. Supplies almost exactly the charter fields this lab asks for, including a named commissioner and an assumptions log.

Day 2 — Getting data you can trust

2.1 · SQL as the lingua franca

Start here · Interactive SQL lessons
SQLBolt — sqlbolt.com · ~30 min
The six verbs, in the browser, no signup and nothing to install. The first four lessons are enough for this session.

2.2 · The laptop warehouse

Start here · Official documentation
DuckDB — duckdb.org · ~20 min
The CSV and Parquet guides are the parts that matter. Note that /docs/stable/ redirects here.

2.3 · Excel as the bridge

Start here · Spreadsheet Horror Stories
EuSpRIG — European Spreadsheet Risks Interest Group · ~10 min
Documented cases from 1995 to 2024, including 16,000 Covid cases lost to a row limit and $92m lost to a mistyped date.

2.4 · Joins that lie

Start here · Understand star schema and the importance for Power BI
Microsoft — Microsoft Learn · ~18 min
Defines fact-table grain and one-to-many cardinality in prose, with no code. The hook for the fan-out demonstration.

Go deeper · Kimball Dimensional Modeling Techniques
Kimball Group — Drawn from The Data Warehouse Toolkit, 3rd ed. (2013) · ~25 min
‘Declaring the Grain’ and ‘Basic Fact Table Techniques’. Download a copy — the hosting is the weak point, not the content.

Also · Understanding symmetric aggregates
Google Cloud — Looker documentation · ~7 min
A one-to-many join where SUM returns 223.44 instead of 124.84. This session’s title in a single table.

2.5 · Data quality as a contract

Start here · Data tests
dbt — dbt documentation · ~15 min
The concrete form of ‘from please check the data to a report that passes or fails’ — unique, not_null, accepted_values, relationships.

2.6 · Lab 2 · The pipeline that reruns

Start here · Data tests
dbt — dbt documentation · ~15 min
Re-read before the lab. A test returns failing rows; zero rows means it passed.

Day 3 — Seeing, forecasting, segmenting

3.1 · What is odd here?

Start here · Exploratory data analysis
Wickham, Çetinkaya-Rundel & Grolemund — R for Data Science, 2nd ed., ch. 10 · ~30 min
Variation, unusual values, covariation. ‘EDA is not a formal process with a strict set of rules. More than anything, EDA is a state of mind.’

3.2 · Charts that survive a board room

Start here · Fundamentals of Data Visualization
Wilke — Complete author manuscript, CC BY-NC-ND · ~35 min
Chapter 6 on amounts and chapters 7 to 9 on distributions — the direct match for the bar chart that hides its own data.

Go deeper · Same Stats, Different Graphs
Matejka & Fitzmaurice — CHI 2017 · ~10 min
The Datasaurus Dozen. Twelve datasets, identical summary statistics to two decimal places, wildly different shapes.

3.3 · Forecasting with intervals

Start here · Forecasting: Principles and Practice
Hyndman & Athanasopoulos — 3rd edition, free online · ~45 min
Chapter 3 on decomposition and chapter 5 on the forecaster’s toolbox, which covers naive benchmarks and prediction intervals.

3.4 · Segmentation you can act on

Start here · Step 8: Selecting the Target Segment(s)
Dolnicar, Grün & Leisch — Market Segmentation Analysis, ch. 10 (open access, CC BY 4.0) · ~25 min
The step most people skip: scoring a segment on attractiveness and on whether you can actually serve it. Skip the R and it reads as a management chapter.

Go deeper · Step 5: Extracting Segments
Dolnicar, Grün & Leisch — Market Segmentation Analysis, ch. 7 (open access) · ~30 min
k-means, standardisation, and the honest answer on choosing k — pick the number that reproduces stably and that the business can act on.

3.5 · Propensity and the leak

Go deeper · Rules of Machine Learning: Best Practices for ML Engineering
Zinkevich — Google Developers · ~40 min
Rules 29 to 37 on training–serving skew are the industrial version of the leakage problem this session dramatises.

3.6 · Lab 3 · The forecast market

Start here · Prediction Markets in Theory and Practice
Wolfers & Zitzewitz — NBER Working Paper 12083 (2006) · ~25 min
Why a contract price is a probability forecast — the justification for making the room bet rather than opine.

Go deeper · Developing expert political judgment
Chang, Chen, Mellers & Tetlock — Judgment and Decision Making 11(5) (2016), open access · ~50 min
Can forecasting be taught? Training improved Brier scores by 6 to 11 per cent in all four tournament years.

Also · Forecast Verification: Issues, Methods and FAQ
WMO Joint Working Group — jwgfvr.github.io · ~15 min
The canonical free definitions of the Brier score and reliability diagrams. This is how the lab is scored.

Day 4 — Unstructured data, reproducible reporting and the executive narrative

4.1 · Text as data

Start here · ChatGPT Outperforms Crowd-Workers for Text-Annotation Tasks
Gilardi, Alizadeh & Kubli — arXiv 2303.15056 (2023) · ~20 min
A model treated as a measurement instrument: the whole argument runs on agreement statistics against a human-labelled benchmark.

Go deeper · Text as Data: The Promise and Pitfalls of Automatic Content Analysis
Grimmer & Stewart — Political Analysis 21(3) (2013) · ~80 min
The canonical statement: no method is assumption-free, and all automated classification must be validated against human labels.

Also · Interrater reliability: the kappa statistic
McHugh — Biochemia Medica 22(3) (2012) · ~15 min
Six pages that let a non-coder read a kappa table properly, and know when not to trust one.

4.2 · Dashboards that get used

Start here · How to set performance metrics for your service
Government Digital Service — GOV.UK Service Manual · ~15 min
Decision-first discipline written by a government rather than a software vendor. The hypothesis template transfers straight to a P&L.

Go deeper · What Do We Talk About When We Talk About Dashboards?
Sarikaya, Correll, Bartram, Tory & Fisher — IEEE TVCG (2019) · ~45 min
Peer-reviewed and taxonomic. The section on problems is the closest thing to a credible free account of why dashboards fail.

4.3 · One source, five outputs

Start here · Parameters
Quarto — Official documentation · ~15 min
Rendering the same document many times with different values — the twelve-regions problem, solved once.

4.4 · The engine under the PDF

Start here · PDF Engine
Quarto — Official documentation · ~12 min
The LaTeX engines, and why TinyTeX exists: roughly 100 MB rather than several gigabytes.

Go deeper · Typst
Quarto — Official documentation · ~20 min
‘As powerful as LaTeX while being much easier to learn.’ Quarto ships the Typst CLI, so there is nothing separate to install.

4.5 · The executive narrative

Start here · Plain language guides
Digital.gov — US federal guidance · ~20 min
Writing for a specific reader, which is what the one-number brief is. Note that plainlanguage.gov now redirects here.

4.6 · Lab 4 · The one-number brief

Start here · Making Data Meaningful, Part 1: writing stories about numbers
UNECE — 2009 · ~25 min
Built around exactly this lab’s problem: one number, a reader with no time, and a conclusion that must arrive in the first line.

Go deeper · Writing about data
UK Government Analysis Function — Updated 2025 · ~12 min
A checklist to hold beside your own draft, including a 25-word sentence ceiling and the inverted pyramid.

Day 5 — Proof, governance and the capstone

5.1 · Correlation, contribution, cause

Start here · The Effect: An Introduction to Research Design and Causality
Huntington-Klein — Free online edition · ~40 min
Chapter 5 on identification and chapter 10 on treatment effects. The most readable causal text for people who are not economists.

Go deeper · Causal Inference: The Mixtape
Cunningham — Free online, now carrying the second edition as The Remix · ~45 min
Code in both R and Stata. Read alongside The Effect rather than instead of it.

5.2 · Experiments that settle arguments

Start here · Diagnosing Sample Ratio Mismatch in A/B Testing
Microsoft Research — Summarising Fabijan et al., KDD 2019 · ~20 min
The taxonomy and rules of thumb behind the thirty-second check. The underlying ACM paper is paywalled; this is the substance without it.

5.3 · When you cannot randomise

Start here · Difference-in-Differences
Huntington-Klein — The Effect, ch. 18 · ~35 min
Parallel trends explained as an assumption you argue for rather than test.

Go deeper · Synthetic Control
Cunningham — Causal Inference: The Mixtape, chs. 9 to 11 · ~60 min
Difference-in-differences fundamentals, complex designs, and synthetic control, with worked code.

5.4 · Governance you can actually run

Start here · NDP Act General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) 2025
Nigeria Data Protection Commission — NDPC/NDP ACT-GAID/01/2025 — 52 articles, 10 schedules · ~60 min
The primary source, from the regulator’s own site. GAID is the General Application and Implementation Directive — nothing to do with generative AI.

Also · Resources
Nigeria Data Protection Commission — The Act itself, the Strategic Roadmap, and Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo translations · ~10 min
Where to find the Act and the Commission’s own guidance notices.

Go deeper · Model Cards for Model Reporting
Mitchell et al. — arXiv 1810.03993 (2018) · ~30 min
The origin of the model card, and the template most organisations still copy.

5.5 · The operating model

Start here · Organizational Models for Big Data and Analytics
Grossman & Siegel — Journal of Organization Design 3(1) (2014), CC BY-NC-ND · ~20 min
Six pages. Frames centralise-versus-embed as a portfolio and governance decision rather than an org-chart preference.

Go deeper · Managing Embedded Data Science Teams for Success
Stelmaszak & Kline — Harvard Data Science Review 5.2 (2023), CC BY 4.0 · ~30 min
Nineteen interviews inside an embedded team. Names the failure modes specifically enough to recognise your own organisation.

5.6 · Capstone · Defence

Start here · How to communicate uncertainty
Africa Check, Chequeado & Full Fact — 2020 · ~25 min
Phrasings you can rehearse: give a range alongside the point estimate, and pair the word with the number — ‘likely (66% chance)’.

Go deeper · Communicating uncertainty about facts, numbers and science
van der Bles, van der Linden, Freeman, Mitchell, Galvão, Zaval & Spiegelhalter — Royal Society Open Science 6(5) (2019), CC BY 4.0 · ~70 min
Does admitting uncertainty cost you credibility? Done well, generally not — which is the reassurance you need before a panel.

Also · How to communicate effectively with policymakers
Cairney & Kwiatkowski — Palgrave Communications 3:37 (2017), open access · ~25 min
The best free account of why a technically correct recommendation loses the room.

How this list was built

58 readings across 30 sessions. Every URL was fetched and confirmed: anything that returned a 404, redirected somewhere unrelated, or turned out to sit behind a paywall or a registration wall was discarded and replaced. Four of the addresses we started from were wrong, which is the argument for checking rather than trusting — a habit this programme spends five days on.

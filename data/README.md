# The data room

Twelve CSV datasets and two deliberately badly-laid-out Excel workbooks. All synthetic, all
generated by [`build/make_data.py`](../build/make_data.py) with a fixed seed.

**The twelve CSVs rebuild byte-for-byte** — regenerate them on any machine and `cmp` reports
no difference, which is what makes every number quoted in this course checkable. The two
`.xlsx` files do not: openpyxl stamps a creation time into the archive, so their bytes change
on every build even though their contents do not.

**Provenance, stated plainly.** No file here contains any real customer, employee,
supplier or company record. The structures, base rates and seasonality are modelled on
what these systems look like in practice across Nigerian and pan-African businesses, but
every row is drawn from a random number generator. Nothing here needs a data-sharing
agreement, and nothing here should ever be presented as evidence about a real market.

**Provenance, stated plainly, part two.** `ng_macro_monthly.csv` is *synthetic macro data*.
Its inflation, policy rate and exchange-rate series are modelled to behave like Nigeria's —
the same order of magnitude, the same broad turning points, an AR(1) structure, a June 2023
float step — but they are **not** the actual published figures from the NBS or the CBN, and no
number in that file should ever be quoted as a fact about the Nigerian economy. It exists so
that "is that real or nominal?" has a defensible answer inside the course, not so that anybody
can cite it. If you need the real series, go to the NBS CPI release and the CBN statistics
portal.

**The data room is salted — unevenly.** Thirteen deliberate defects across the set: a join key
that silently duplicates rows, a feature that leaks the answer, a structural break a forecast
will ignore, a definition that contradicts finance, a filter that removed the angriest
customers.

They are **not one per file.** One file carries four on its own; several carry none at all and
are there precisely so that a clean file feels different from a dirty one; and the two
workbooks have no planted defect of any kind — their difficulty is their layout, which is what
real workbooks are like.

Which file holds which is in `facilitator/salt-key.md` and nowhere else, including here. That
is deliberate: a participant who knows three files are clean checks eleven files instead of
fourteen, and the discipline being taught is to check every file the same way.

An AI copilot will produce clean, confident, well-formatted code against these files and be
wrong. Finding out why is the skill.

The answers are in `facilitator/salt-key.md`, and the twelve gallery briefs are worked in
`facilitator/gallery-solutions.md`. Neither is linked from the participant site. Participants
should not read either before Friday.

A note on the descriptions below: they say what each file **is** — its grain, its period, what
business it describes. They do not say what is wrong with it, and they do not point at the
column to look in. That rule is the same one the participant site follows, and an earlier
version of this file broke it in nine places while promising not to.

## `fmcg_sell_out.csv` — 55,474 rows

Weekly sell-out for a consumer-goods distributor: 40 SKUs across 12 depots, Jan 2024 to
Mar 2026 (Q2 2026 is held back for Wednesday's forecast market). The workhorse
dataset — it appears on all five days.

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `week_starting` | date | Monday of the ISO week |
| `depot_code` | text | joins to `fmcg_depots.csv` |
| `sku` | text | joins to `fmcg_products.csv` |
| `units_sold` | number | units sold in the week |
| `unit_price_ngn` | number | actual realised price, after discount |
| `on_promotion` | 0/1 | |
| `discount_pct` | number | 0 when not on promotion |
| `stockout_flag` | 0/1 | depot reported out of stock during the week |
| `revenue_ngn` | number | `units_sold × unit_price_ngn` |

## `fmcg_products.csv` — 40 rows
SKU dimension: category, pack size, unit cost, list price, launch week.

## `fmcg_depots.csv` — 13 rows
Depot dimension: one row per depot. Name, region, state, opening date.

## `bank_customers.csv` — 12,000 rows
Retail banking customer base with a 90-day churn outcome (~10%). Tenure, segment,
balance, digital engagement, transactions, complaints, salary domiciliation, NPS. One row per
customer as at the snapshot date — there is no history.

## `supply_chain_pos.csv` — 9,000 rows
Purchase orders with promised and actual lead times, incoterms, port of entry and an
`on_time` flag. One row per purchase order.

## `b2b_funnel.csv` — 7,400 rows
Lead → MQL → SQL → closed-won for a B2B software business selling across five African
markets. One row per lead. ACV in USD.

## `hr_attrition.csv` — 4,200 rows
Employee record with a 12-month attrition outcome (~18%). Pay, tenure, commute,
engagement, promotion wait, training hours, span of control, work pattern. One row per
employee.

## `customer_reviews.csv` — 4,938 rows
Free-text reviews across six channels — app stores, social, call-centre notes, branch surveys,
WhatsApp — with star ratings, in English and Nigerian Pidgin. The Day 4 text-analytics
corpus.

## `customer_reviews_labelled.csv` — 4,938 rows
The same corpus with `theme`, `sentiment` and `intent` already attached. Classifying 4,938
reviews live, across a room full of laptops, on conference wifi, is not a thing that works — so 4.2
uses these cached labels for the volume analysis and has the room hand-label a small sample
blind to check them. The hand-labelled gold standard for that check
(`customer_reviews_gold_200.csv`) is in `facilitator/`, not here.

## `ng_macro_monthly.csv` — 90 rows
Monthly macro series, Jan 2019 to Jun 2026: headline and food inflation, USD/NGN, policy
rate, composite PMI, Brent. Used to deflate nominal revenue — the difference between
"we grew 43%" and what actually happened.

## `ab_test_checkout.csv` — 115,970 rows
A four-week checkout experiment: one row per user, with arm, segment, device, conversion and
revenue.

## `marketing_spend.csv` — 786 rows
Weekly spend and conversions across six channels, one row per channel per week. Used in
[5.1](../sessions/5-1-correlation-contribution-cause.qmd).

## `board_pack.xlsx` — 3 sheets, 480 rows
A month-end board pack as it actually arrives: three sheets, two title rows above the header
on each, and a currency column stored as text. Used in
[2.3](../sessions/2-3-excel-bridge.qmd).

## `regional_report.xlsx` — 12 data rows
A report laid out for reading rather than computing: title rows, an italic draft warning,
merged region cells, months across the top as columns, a total row in the middle. The
unmerge-and-unpivot exercise in 2.3 runs on it.

## `../outputs/fmcg_weekly.parquet` / `.csv` — 55,260 rows
Not raw data. This is the **reference build**: `fmcg_sell_out.csv` deduplicated to its true
grain, nulls resolved, joined to the depot and product dimensions. It is the fallback for
Wednesday and Thursday, so that a participant whose Tuesday pipeline did not finish can still
do Wednesday's modelling instead of spending the morning catching up. Wednesday's and
Thursday's sessions say so explicitly.

## Rebuilding

```bash
python3 build/make_data.py
```

Requires `numpy` and `pandas`. The seed is `20261012`.

**Do not change the seed casually.** Every number quoted anywhere in the course — in session
pages, in the slide decks, in the participant guide, in the salt key — is computed from this
seed. Changing it moves every salt and silently falsifies several hundred figures in the
teaching materials.

If you do want a fresh dataset for a repeat cohort, the seed is only step one of four:

```bash
# 1. change the seed in build/make_data.py (`RNG = np.random.default_rng(...)`), then:
python3 build/make_data.py            # regenerate the twelve CSVs and two workbooks
python3 build/course_facts.py --write # recompute every number the materials quote
python3 build/verify_salts.py         # 93 assertions: does every salt still bite?
```

Then read the regenerated `facilitator/course-facts.md` and update the figures quoted in the
session pages and decks to match it. `verify_salts.py` will tell you whether the salts survived
— it will not tell you that page 3-1 still says "53 weeks" when the new draw has 52. That part
is manual, it takes about an hour, and skipping it is how a facilitator ends up contradicted by
their own dataset in front of the room.

For most cohorts the right answer is to leave the seed alone. The defects are not secret
because they are hard to find; they are useful because the room finds them live.
